### Study Note: What Are Vats? (Proverbs 3:10)

A **vat** is a large basin or container used to collect freshly pressed grape juice. In ancient Israel, grapes were crushed in a winepress and the juice collected in a basin, sometimes cut into stone.

The Hebrew word translated **"vats"** is **יֶקֶב (*yeqeb*)**, pronounced *YEH-kehb*. It refers to a winepress, trough, or receiving basin for the juice of pressed grapes.

In Proverbs 3:9–10, two images describe an abundant harvest:

- **Barns filled with plenty:** Grain and other harvested produce in abundant supply. As a personal application, this can bring to mind provision, resources, and a household with sufficient supply.
- **Vats overflowing with new wine:** A plentiful grape harvest producing more juice than the vessels can hold. As a personal application, this can bring to mind fruitfulness, increase, and abundance.

The passage connects honoring the LORD with one's possessions and firstfruits to an image of agricultural abundance. The central picture is **a harvest so abundant that its containers cannot hold all its produce**.

My earlier comparisons to bank accounts, home, household, storage, and new ideas are **personal reflections and applications**; the literal meaning of *yeqeb* concerns the winepress or juice-collecting vat.
